Inside the PEG2 model

Heme sits among four peptide helices. Histidine side chains on two helices face its iron center, Fe; guanosines connect through PEG2 linkers.

From color to a peroxide readout

If active, heme would let hydrogen peroxide oxidize ABTS into a green product. Spectrophotometer absorbance, calibrated against known peroxide concentrations, could then provide a concentration readout.

Fe–His bonds are built into these coordinates; they do not establish laboratory binding or activity.

01 / THE QUESTION

Can a small peptide assembly organize hemin for catalysis?

Guanosine-capped peptides offer a way to connect molecular recognition with a compact heme-binding scaffold.

The design asks whether four capped helices can assemble around hemin and support peroxide-dependent oxidation. The molecular model makes that design inspectable. Hemin binding, assembly, and catalytic performance remain experimental questions.

Controls, biosensor connection & sources

What would establish a peroxide assay?

Compare assembly plus hemin with matched free hemin, assembly without hemin, and reagent blanks. Include no-peroxide and sample-background measurements; an established peroxidase can provide a positive control.

Hold buffer, ABTS, peroxide, timing, and hemin concentration constant where applicable. Then measure a calibration against known peroxide, repeatability, stability, selectivity, and interference in the intended sample. This page supplies no assay concentration, wavelength, sensitivity, or performance value for the bundle.

Return to the planned laboratory comparison ↓

Where does the biosensor connection come from?

Hydrogen peroxide can be an analyte itself or the product of another recognition reaction. For example, glucose oxidase produces peroxide during glucose oxidation. A validated peroxidase readout could report that peroxide as an optical signal.

Published heme-enzyme and protein–hemin assays support this general route. Their architectures and measured performance belong to those systems, not to the guanosine-capped bundle.

04

Macwan’s NAMD trajectory

Matched PSF/PDB files were prepared in VMD, solvated with water and KCl, then minimized, heated, equilibrated, and simulated by Prof. Macwan.

This five-second VMD replay shows saved coordinates from that existing run. Rigid alignment removes overall drift. Playback seconds are not physical simulation time; the nanosecond interval has not been verified.

05

Analysis of the saved structures

The reported core stayed compact, with partial helix retention. Persistent guanine-quartet contacts were not supported by the trajectory checks. Fe–His bonds were built into the topology.

Compactness and imposed coordination do not demonstrate spontaneous hemin binding, peroxide oxidation, or sensor performance.

Read the reported analysis ↗
Analysis and interpretation details

These are reported project findings, not a new independent reanalysis. A complete starting guanine quartet was not established; the interpretation is that tested guanine contacts were not sustained. The two Fe–His connections were explicit topology bonds, so their retention cannot establish spontaneous coordination or binding affinity.

The corrected PEG2 reference

A corrected 2HH:2HA_AC solute build passed static checks. It is separate from the original trajectory; a corrected simulation has not been run.

A cleaner starting reference resolves model preparation questions. It does not supply missing dynamics or laboratory results.

Reference-version details

07

The next laboratory comparison

The plan is to synthesize PEG3 His8 and Ala8 variants, compare five HH/HA mixing ratios, and measure assembly and hemin binding. The existing purchased gs-helix(A8H) offers another experimental route.

ABTS oxidation compares assemblies with and without hemin, alongside hemin-only and reagent controls. A sensor claim would then need calibrated peroxide response, selectivity, repeatability, stability, and tests of sample interference.

Open the laboratory plan in OneNote ↗
Planned experiment details

The 2026–2027 goals describe PEG3 helices with histidine at position 8 (HH) and alanine at position 8 (HA), synthesized on amide resin, with staged test cleavages and planned HH/HA mixtures of 100:0, 75:25, 50:50, 25:75, and 0:100. Planned CD, thermal response, hemin binding, and possible catalysis are not completed results. Mixture ratios alone would not establish a defined four-chain assembly.

The laboratory question

Earlier laboratory samples were purified and examined by HPLC, mass spectrometry, and circular dichroism. Those experimental constructs and the historical poster are distinct from this computational PEG2 model. The next comparisons test assembly, hemin binding, and ABTS oxidation; this model does not establish performance. A useful sensor would also need tests of selectivity, repeatability, and sample interference.

Heme enzymes and artificial peroxidases already support peroxide assays. This project tests a specific guanosine-capped helical assembly. The lab’s earlier nanofiber paper describes a different architecture; it does not establish activity for this bundle.
